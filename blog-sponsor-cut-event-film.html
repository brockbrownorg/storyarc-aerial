The Sponsor Cut: Giving Backers a Film That Shows Their Money Working

Your sponsors paid for a stage, a zone, a day of programming, and they were not there for most of it. At renewal time you will ask them to pay again. The film you hand them can make that conversation easy or hard.

May 2026

What a sponsor is actually buying

A sponsor is not buying a logo on a banner. They are buying association with a day people loved, visibility in front of a crowd they want to reach, and a story they can show their own leadership. When all you send after the event is a thank you note and a few phone photos, you leave them to imagine the value. Imagination renews poorly.

A sponsor cut is a short film, usually one to two minutes per major backer or one shared film with clear sections, that shows their funded element working. The stage they paid for, full and alive. The family zone they supported, busy at midday. Their banner in frame as the crowd arrives, seen at scale from the air and at eye level from the ground.

Handled well, it also gives the sponsor something to post themselves, which puts your event in front of their audience at no cost to you. That second use is often worth as much as the first.

Plan sponsor shots before the day, not after

The sponsor cut fails when it is assembled from whatever happened to be filmed. It succeeds when it is planned like any other obligation in your run sheet.

  • List every sponsor promise in one place. Logo placement, naming, stage mentions, hospitality, and any film or photo deliverable you agreed to.
  • Assign each promise a moment. Arrivals for banners, peak sets for stages, quiet morning light for a funded garden or market row before crowds thicken.
  • Agree on approval and usage in writing. Who may post the cut, where, and for how long belongs in the quote and in your sponsor agreement.
  • Deliver while gratitude is warm. Finished films within five business days is our standard, and sponsor cuts should ride that same clock, not arrive at renewal time.

If a promise cannot be filmed as written, for example a banner placed where crowds make aerial filming unwise, you want to know before the event, while you can move the banner or change the plan. After the event, that conversation is an apology.

How the aerial view helps, and where it stops

Scale is the sponsor story the ground cannot tell. A wide aerial frame from the edge of the grounds shows the sponsored stage, the crowd in front of it filmed from a distance, and the rest of your event stretching beyond. It proves reach in one image.

Our practice over crowds is restraint, and sponsors should hear it as a strength. We do not fly over guests. We launch from clear areas, work from the perimeter, and use height and lens to include crowds without placing an aircraft above them. In Omaha, sites near downtown and the RiverFront also need airspace and venue checks shaped by Eppley Airfield, and that planning is free and done in advance.

The honest limits of a sponsor film

A good sponsor cut cannot renew a sponsor whose on site experience was poor. If their tent leaked, their access was blocked, or their name was said wrong from the stage, film will not repair it, and overselling the video in that meeting can hurt your credibility. Fix the experience first, then let the film prove the fix next year.

There is a second limit. If your sponsorships are small and numerous, a custom film for every backer will cost more in edit time than the renewals justify. In that case one strong shared sponsor film, plus still frames pulled for each backer, is the honest buy. We will recommend that version when the numbers point that way, even though it is a smaller sale for us.

Tie it to next year from the start

The sponsor cut and the public recap come from the same flights and the same day, but they do different jobs in different rooms. That is why we plan them together, as set out in your recap video is next year's ticket sales team. One library of footage, cut for the fan who has not bought yet and for the backer deciding whether to fund again.

Every written quote from us lists deliverables, delivery date, and usage rights, including which sponsor cuts are included and what an extra custom cut costs. Ask any operator you consider for the same list, in writing, before the event. Sponsors notice professionalism in the small things, and a clear film plan is one of the small things that reads as a well run event.

What to send a sponsor, and when

Send the cut with a short note that names what their money funded, thanks the people who did the work on the ground, and states one clear hope for next year. Keep the ask itself for a later meeting unless your agreement already sets the renewal rhythm. A film delivered fast, with usage rights clear enough for their own team to share it, does quiet work for weeks before that meeting happens. Sponsors remember who made them look good in front of their own leadership, and they remember who left them explaining a blurry phone photo instead.
