Where a Flythrough Earns Its Keep: Homepage, Google Profile, Booking Page, Social

The same film can be a booking asset or an expensive ornament, and the difference is rarely the film. It is where you put it. A flythrough earns in the places a buyer is deciding, and it fades in the places a crowd is merely scrolling.

By Brock Brown, founder and pilot, StoryArc Aerial · November 2026

Rank placements by decision, not by audience size

Venue teams often launch a new film on social media because that is where applause is visible, then wonder why enquiries did not move. Social reach is the largest audience and the weakest intent. A person scrolling at night is not comparing venues. A person on your booking page with a date in mind is. Placement should follow intent, and intent has a clear order.

Think of each placement as a job interview question. On the homepage the film must answer: is this the kind of place we want? On the Google profile it must answer: of the five venues in this search, which looks real? On the booking page it must answer: should I commit to the tour, the date, the room? In the enquiry reply it must answer a specific person doubts, personally. On social it must answer only: is this worth a second look? One film, cut and framed slightly differently, can hold all five jobs.

The four placements that pay

  • Homepage, above the gallery. The flythrough should be the first moving image a visitor meets, set where hero photographs usually sit, because it does the hero job better: place, scale, and feeling in under two minutes, or in a muted looping opening section if your design uses one.
  • Google profile, pinned and current. Local search is where Omaha and metro venues are actually shortlisted, from the Old Market to Papillion and Elkhorn, often by people who will never browse your social feed. A current flythrough on the profile works at the exact moment of comparison.
  • Booking and tours page, beside the request button. This is the highest intent square footage you own. The visitor is deciding whether to give you their details. The film beside that decision is not decoration, it is the last piece of evidence.
  • The enquiry reply. A link in the first response, framed in one line: this is the walk you would take on a visit. It reaches decision makers who never enquired, the partner, parent, planner, or director the film gets forwarded to.

Social media: useful, but know its rank

Social cutdowns have a real job. They remind past enquirers you exist, they give planners and partners something easy to forward, and they keep a venue present in the long gaps between booking seasons. Short sections work best: the arrival, the main room reveal, the terrace at evening, each cut as its own small promise rather than the whole film compressed.

But here is the honest ranking a vendor should give you: if your flythrough lives only on social media, it will almost certainly not repay its cost, because the audience least likely to book is the only one who saw it. We would rather you place the master film properly in four quiet places than promote it loudly in one wrong one. Views are the metric social platforms report. Qualified tours are the metric your venue should report.

Placement mistakes that waste a good film

The commonest mistake is the gallery burial: the film uploaded to a media page under twelve photograph albums, where no deciding visitor ever scrolls. The second is the stale film: a route showing a room you renovated last spring, still live on the profile, teaching buyers to distrust everything else you publish. A flythrough should be reviewed whenever the building changes, and replaced or retired without sentimentality.

The third is the orphaned film: a beautiful video with no next step beside it. Every placement should sit next to the action it supports, tour request, enquiry form, date checker, or a clear contact route. A film that raises desire and offers nowhere for the desire to go is a gift to the next venue on the shortlist.

Seasonal timing matters locally. Venue searches surge in engagement season, ahead of summer events, and around College World Series weeks when Omaha fills with visitors planning returns and reunions. A flythrough placed and checked before those surges earns at the moments your competitors are busiest and least able to answer clearly.

A simple placement audit you can do today

Open your own site as a stranger. Time how many clicks it takes to see your space move. Check your Google profile on a phone, the way a searcher sees it. Read your last enquiry reply and ask whether the recipient could picture the venue an hour later. If any of those fail, the fix costs nothing but attention, and it will do more for the film you already own than any new shoot.
