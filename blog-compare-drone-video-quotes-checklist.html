How to Compare Drone Video Quotes: The Line by Line Checklist

Two quotes can be thousands of dollars apart and both be honest, because the totals are hiding different scopes. The only safe way to choose is to stop comparing totals and start comparing lines, one decision at a time, until every quote is answering the same questions.

November 2025

Put every quote into the same rows

Take a blank page and write the same rows for each quote you hold. If a quote does not answer a row, write the words not stated in that space. Those blanks are the most useful information in the whole exercise, because an unstated item is an item you are carrying the risk on.

  • Deliverables. What films, clips, or files, in what lengths and formats?
  • Edit scope. Music, color, titles, and social cutdowns included, or flight footage only?
  • Time on site. How many hours or how much of a day, and what happens if the shoot runs long?
  • Planning. Flight planning, shot list, and a walk of the space before the day, or arrival and improvisation?
  • Permissions. Who completes the location and airspace check, and who asks the venue?
  • Usage rights. Where you may run the film, for how long, stated in writing or left unsaid?
  • Delivery date. A date you can build a launch around, or no date at all?
  • Weather terms. What happens, and what it costs, if wind or rain moves the day?
  • Revisions. How many rounds of changes are included before extra fees begin?

Price the rows that are missing

A missing row is rarely free. It simply gets paid for later, by you, in a less convenient currency. If the edit is missing, you will pay an editor or spend your own hours. If social cutdowns are missing, you will pay to have the master film recut for each platform. If usage rights are missing, you may pay again when you want to run the film in an ad, or worse, find you cannot. If revisions are missing, the first round of changes becomes a negotiation instead of a step in the process.

Add a rough cost or a rough risk to each blank before you compare totals. You do not need exact figures. You need to see the shape. A quote that looks hundreds of dollars cheaper can become the most expensive route once the editor, the recuts, and the rights conversation are added. A quote that looks expensive can turn out to be the complete price, with nothing waiting behind it. That second shape is usually the safer buy for a business planning a launch date around the film.

Check the rows that protect a date

Some rows matter more in Omaha than a national checklist would admit. Nebraska wind is a real planning factor in every season, and summer storm cells can move across the metro in an afternoon. For an event tied to a fixed date, like coverage during College World Series weeks when the whole city is full, a missing weather and backup plan is not a small gap. Ask what the operator does if the morning brings high wind. Ask who makes the call, when they make it, and whether rescheduling costs you money.

Location belongs in the same check. A site outside Papillion and a site in the Old Market near Eppley Airfield raise different airspace and permission questions. A quote that never asked for your address has not actually priced your shoot. It has priced a generic shoot, and generic quotes are where surprise fees are born. The location and airspace check is free with us, and any operator you choose should be willing to look at your specific place before holding you to a number.

Decide on the job, not the total

Once the rows are filled, decide what job you are buying. If you have an editor and need only clean aerial clips, a flight only scope is a legitimate choice and the lower quote may serve you well. If you need a finished film on your homepage by a set date, with rights you can rely on, choose the quote that states all of that in writing, even if it is not the lowest number in the pile.

The sentence that costs us something: if a competitor quote fills every row on this checklist, states its rights and dates plainly, and still comes in under ours, that is a good quote and you should probably take it. We would rather lose a job to a complete quote than win one against a blank page. Send any quote to us through the quote page and we will apply this same checklist to it, marking what it leaves out, and we will include our own quote in that review if you ask us to. That is the whole point of the review. It works the same in both directions.

One last habit separates confident buyers from anxious ones. They keep the comparison in one place, a single page or a single document, rather than in memory and inbox threads. Quotes change as conversations happen, and a note added in a call should be dated and added to the row it changes. When the final choice is questioned later, by a partner, a board, or your own future self, the page explains it. Decisions documented this way age well. Decisions remembered this way rarely do, because memory edits totals and forgets blanks.
