What a Brand Film Costs in the Midwest: Honest Ranges and What Moves Them

Ask what a brand film costs and you will hear everything from a few hundred dollars to numbers that sound like a house. Most of that spread is not dishonesty. It is people pricing different films under the same name. This page shows how the price is actually built, so you can judge any quote, including ours.

By Brock Brown, founder and pilot, StoryArc Aerial · November 2026

There is no honest flat price, and here is why

A brand film is not a product off a shelf. It is a filming day or several, in one place or several, cut into one film or a library, for a homepage or for a campaign. Anyone who gives a firm total before asking about locations, deliverables, and usage is guessing with your money. What can be published honestly are the building blocks, and a serious quote is simply those blocks assembled in writing for your project.

Here are the blocks, from published figures on this site. A single location FPV flythrough, the aerial tour of one place with a finished master film and short social cutdowns, starts from $1,250, with most single location flythroughs landing between $1,250 and $2,500. Production day blocks from story led operators in this region are published at $850 for a half day and $1,650 for a full day, while national production support commonly runs between $1,500 and $2,000 and up per day. A brand film quote is built by combining elements like these with the edit, the locations, and the deliverables your project actually needs.

Read that again carefully, because it is the whole method: we are not publishing a brand film price list, and you should be wary of anyone who does. We are showing the honest units a quote is assembled from, so your total can be checked rather than merely believed.

The five factors that move your total

  • Filming time. A half day in one place is a different film from two full days across a metro. Time is the largest lever in almost every quote.
  • Locations. Each added location adds travel, setup, permissions, and light planning. A campus in west Omaha and a second site in Bellevue in one day is possible, and it is not free.
  • Deliverables. One hero film costs less than a hero film plus recruiting, testimonial, and social cuts. A library is better value per piece and a larger total, and both facts belong in the quote.
  • People and process. Interviews, scripted voice, employees or customers on camera, and multiple approval rounds all add planning and edit time.
  • Usage and turnaround. Where the film runs and for how long belongs in writing, and a rush ahead of a fixed launch or a College World Series week crowd will cost more than a standard schedule.

Omaha buyers hold one genuine advantage. Midwest day rates sit below national production support, travel inside the metro is short, and a film planned around Nebraska light and seasons rarely needs the location spending a coastal shoot absorbs. The saving is real, and it comes from geography and planning, not from cutting the work.

How to read any brand film quote

Ask for the blocks. A quote you can trust names the filming time, the locations, the deliverables, the delivery date, and the usage rights, line by line. Every written quote from us lists deliverables, delivery date, and usage rights, and finished films are delivered within five business days on a standard schedule. Hold every bidder to that same list and the cheapest and the most expensive quotes will finally be describing comparable work, or you will see exactly where they are not.

Our services page shows the brand story work these quotes describe, and the homepage film guide shows what the finished film should accomplish in its first ten seconds. Read both before a budget meeting and you will walk in knowing which deliverable matters most to you, which is the strongest position a buyer can be in.

One comparison to keep out of the room: a quick real estate style listing package, which runs between $95 and $500 locally, is a different and commodity product. It is honest work for a different job, and comparing it with a brand film will only confuse a decision that deserves clarity.

The honest floor, stated plainly

If your true budget sits below the flythrough range above, a produced brand film is not the right buy yet, and we would rather say that here than take the money and deliver a thin version of the film you imagined. A business at that stage is usually better served by strong ground level video of its people, a well kept Google profile, and saving the produced film for the season when the budget can carry it properly. A cheap brand film that looks cheap costs more than no film, because it spends your credibility along with your money.

At the other end, a multi location, multi day library for a full campaign will legitimately cost several times a single location piece. Judged per finished asset and per month of use, it is often the best value on this page. The question is never the total alone. It is what the total is built from, what you receive, and how long it keeps working. Get those in writing, and the price conversation becomes a planning conversation, which is where good films come from.
