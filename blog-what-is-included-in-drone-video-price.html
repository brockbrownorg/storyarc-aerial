What Is Included in a Drone Video Price, and What Is Extra

The total on a quote is only half the information. The other half is the boundary: what that total includes, and where the extras begin. Buyers get hurt at that boundary, not at the total. Here is where the line normally sits, so you can check any quote against it.

January 2026

What a complete price normally includes

  • Planning. The conversation, the flight path or shot list, and the scheduling work that turns an idea into a shoot day.
  • The location and airspace check. With us it is free, and it happens before the quote is final. Your address is checked as your address, whether that is an open site near Gretna or a downtown location near Eppley Airfield.
  • Venue permission coordination. The asking and confirming that lets filming happen legitimately at your place.
  • The filming time in the agreed block. A flythrough visit of one to two hours for most single locations, or the half day or full day block you booked for coverage.
  • The finished edit. Selection, ordering, color, and music for the master film.
  • The named cutdowns. Social versions included in the scope, in the shapes the quote lists.
  • Usage rights as stated. Where the film may run and for how long, in writing.
  • Delivery by a stated date. Finished films within five business days as our standard, with the actual date in the quote.

For flythroughs, that bundle is what sits inside the published shape of from $1,250, with most single location projects between $1,250 and $2,500. The full cost guide shows how the same logic applies across brand films, events, and production day blocks.

What is normally extra, and should be priced as an option

  • Rush delivery. Compressing the edit into a day or two costs more because the hours stack. Fair, when named in advance.
  • Raw files. The camera files for your own editor are a separate deliverable with separate handling, priced as their own line.
  • Extra locations and extra days. A second venue, a return visit in another season, or a production day beyond the block.
  • Extra versions. Additional edits for other audiences, other languages of captions, or a sponsor specific cut beyond the agreed set.
  • Wider usage. Paid advertising, broadcast, or use by additional businesses beyond the original grant.
  • Additional revision rounds. Beyond the rounds the quote includes.

None of these extras is a trick. Each is real work or real added value. The only version that should worry you is the extra you learn about after the shoot, when your choices have narrowed. An extra priced as an option in the written quote is a decision you control. The same item surfacing later is a fee you absorb.

Two boundary cases worth settling in advance

Weather sits on the boundary for many operators, and it should not. Nebraska wind and summer storms are foreseeable here, so the rescheduling terms belong inside the standard price conversation. Ours is plain and printed: weather rescheduling never punishes the buyer. If a quote charges for weather moves, that policy should be visible before you sign, priced into your decision like any other line.

Travel is the other quiet boundary. A metro that runs from Bellevue to Elkhorn and out to Gretna covers real distance, and event work can reach further across Nebraska and Iowa. Ask whether travel inside your area is included and where it becomes a line item. Again, either answer can be fair. A surprise cannot.

The one paragraph that protects you

Every written quote should end the guessing with three lists stated together: the deliverables included, the extras and their prices, and the exclusions. Ours also restates delivery date and usage rights, because those are the two lines buyers re read most often in the months after a shoot, when a sponsor asks for a cut or a campaign wants the film in an ad.

Our honesty line: sometimes reading this boundary carefully will show you that a simpler product fits your need, including the local real estate listing packages that run $95 to $500 and are a different, commodity product from ours. If a quick listing set is all your project requires, buying our included bundle is overpaying, and we will point you that way.

Bring any quote you hold to this list, and bring your own project to the quote page for a quote with the boundary drawn in writing from the start.

When you review a quote, try one simple exercise. Point at each line and say aloud what would happen if it vanished. If planning vanished, the day would improvise. If the check vanished, permissions would become your problem. If the edit vanished, you would own files instead of a film. Any line whose disappearance changes nothing is padding and can be challenged. Any line whose disappearance moves real work or real risk onto you is not an extra at all, whatever it is called. This exercise takes five minutes, works on any quote from any operator, and turns a vague feeling about a total into a specific conversation about value.

A useful question for any line you cannot classify is simply this: is it included, is it an option, or is it excluded? Three possible answers, and an operator should be able to give one of them for every line without hesitation. A line that draws a long pause has just told you where your surprise was going to live.
