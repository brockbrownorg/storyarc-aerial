What We Need From You Before a Shoot (It Is Less Than You Think)

Many first time buyers delay booking because they imagine a mountain of preparation waiting on their side of the project. There is not one. The planning, the checks, and the flight work belong to us. Your list fits on an index card, and here it is in full.

December 2025

The short list, complete

  • The address. The location and airspace check runs on your specific place, and it is free. A site outside Elkhorn and a site downtown near Eppley Airfield need different checks, so the address comes first and decides much of the plan.
  • The outcome in your words. What should this film do? Book tours, explain the space, impress a sponsor, fill a homepage? Two or three sentences guide every later decision better than any technical brief.
  • Access on the day. Someone who can unlock the space, or a code, or a name at the front desk. Flythrough work often happens in a quiet window before opening or between bookings, and smooth access protects that window.
  • One contact person. One phone number for the day itself, for the small questions that always arise, like where to park or which door to use.
  • A tidy version of the space. Not a remodel. The camera sees clutter honestly, so clear the stray boxes, the cleaning cart, and the cars that would not be in your own brochure photo. Normal, lived in, and tidy beats staged and strange.
  • Permission from whoever controls the space. If you own or manage the venue, that is you. If you are filming at a property someone else runs, their yes needs to exist before the day, and we can help you ask for it in the right words.

That is the whole list. Everything else people brace for, the shot list, the flight path, the weather watch, the timing of light, the permissions research, belongs to us. Our Omaha cost guide shows how the quote is built around that division of labor, which is why the written quote can state deliverables, delivery date, and usage rights without a page of homework attached to it.

What is not on your list

You do not need a script. You do not need to know what FPV means beyond what our guides explain, and you do not need to direct camera moves. You do not need to clear the whole building, hire security, or close for the day for an ordinary flythrough, which typically films in one to two hours inside a normal working schedule. You do not need perfect weather luck either. Nebraska wind and fast summer weather are our planning problem, we watch the forecast as the day approaches, and weather rescheduling never punishes the buyer if the day has to move.

You also do not need to have every marketing decision made. Buyers sometimes hold a booking hostage to questions the shoot does not depend on, like the final wording of a homepage headline. The film can be planned around the places it will most likely live, your site, your Google profile, your social channels, and the details can settle while the edit does.

Three small things that quietly help a lot

None of these is required. All three punch above their weight. First, tell us about timing traps: a wedding venue outside Bellevue with a Saturday booking, a gym in Papillion at peak hour, a dealership with a delivery arriving mid morning. Knowing the rhythm of your day lets us choose the calm window instead of colliding with your busiest one. Second, mention the features you are proudest of, even if you think they are obvious. The room, the view, the scale of the yard, the light in the morning. Pride is a reliable guide to what the camera should find. Third, if people will appear in the space, give them a simple heads up that filming is happening. A relaxed room films better than a surprised one, every time.

The honesty note: occasionally a space is not ready to be filmed well, because a renovation is half done, a season is wrong for the grounds, or the honest priority is fixing the booking page the film would point to. In those cases we will say wait, even though waiting delays our own booking. A film of a space at its best is worth more than a film next week of a space mid repair.

When your short list is ready, send it through the quote page. The questionnaire takes about two minutes, and most of it is the address and the outcome you already know.

Buyers sometimes ask whether they should be present for the whole shoot. For most flythroughs, being reachable matters more than being present. Say hello at the start if you can, point out anything we should know that only someone who works in the space would know, and then let your day proceed. For events, a short check in at the moments that matter most to you is plenty. Your presence is a gift of local knowledge, not a duty, and the shoot is planned to succeed with the short list above rather than with your constant supervision standing behind the camera.
