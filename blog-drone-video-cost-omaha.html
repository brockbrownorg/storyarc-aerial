What Does Drone Video Actually Cost in Omaha? An Honest Guide

Send the same project to three drone operators and you may get quotes of $600, $2,400, and $7,500 back. Usually all three are honest. They are just describing three different jobs. This guide shows you the real ranges, what moves a price, and how to read a quote line by line.

The short answer, by project type

These are the ranges Omaha buyers actually plan around, drawn from published local and regional pricing. Your project lands somewhere inside them based on the five factors further down.

FPV flythrough for a venue or business

From $1,250, with most single location flythroughs landing between $1,250 and $2,500. Includes flight planning, the filming visit, a finished master film, and short social cutdowns.

Brand and business films with aerial

Quoted per project. Aerial sequences inside a brand film are priced around the film day, the locations, and the edit. Expect a written scope rather than an hourly rate.

Event coverage

Priced by the half day or full day. You are buying coverage of an immovable date, plus a recap film and sponsor ready cuts after it. Bigger events with multiple deliverables sit at the top of the range.

Action and motorsports coverage

Priced by the event day. The quote states what is included: the flights, the finish, the cuts, and the delivery date.

An aerial unit for a production company

Quoted by the day block. Story led operators in this region publish $850 for a half day and $1,650 for a full day; national production support commonly runs $1,500 to $2,000 and up per day. Editing is usually an add on, quoted up front.

Real estate listing photos and clips

$95 to $500 locally. This is the commodity end of the market: quick listing packages, next day delivery, priced per property. It is a different product from the films above, and we will happily point you to it if that is all you need.

The five things that move a quote

  • Location and airspace. A farm field outside Gretna is simple. Downtown near Eppley Airfield or a RiverFront venue means authorizations and permissions, and the planning time to secure them.
  • Time on site. Most flythroughs film in one to two hours. Event coverage can run a full day. Time is the biggest single lever.
  • Raw footage or finished film. Raw files for your editor cost less than a finished film with music, color, and social cutdowns. Make sure you know which one a quote describes.
  • Turnaround. Standard delivery is measured in business days. Same day or next day edits for an event cost more because someone is editing while the day is still news.
  • Usage. Where the film will run, and for how long, belongs in the quote in writing. A film you can use everywhere forever is worth more than one licensed for a single post.

Why the three quotes were all different

The $600 quote usually covers showing up and flying: raw clips, no edit, no social cuts, no usage terms in writing, and the permissions left to you. The $2,400 quote usually describes the finished product: planning, a legal flight, an edited film, cutdowns, and rights you can actually use. The $7,500 quote is typically a production: multiple days or locations, a crew, and a campaign level edit. None of them is the scam. Comparing them as if they were the same job is the trap.

Before you choose, ask every operator the same five questions and compare the answers, not the totals:

  • Is the edit included, and what exactly do I receive?
  • Are the airspace check and venue permissions handled, and included?
  • What usage rights do I get, in writing?
  • What is the delivery date, and what happens if the weather turns?
  • What is not included that I might assume is?

What a written quote should list

Ours lists the flights, the deliverables, the delivery date, and your usage rights, line by line, and so should anyone's. If a quote is one number and a handshake, you are the one carrying the risk. A professional would rather put it in writing than surprise you later. That is the standard to hold every quote to, including ours.
