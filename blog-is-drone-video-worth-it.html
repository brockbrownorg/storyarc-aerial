Is Drone Video Worth It for a Business Like Yours? An Honest Answer

The honest answer is: it depends entirely on what you sell. When what you sell is a place, a day, or a sense of scale, aerial video is one of the hardest working assets a business can buy. When you sell a conversation, it is usually a pretty expense. Here is how to tell which one you are.

Where aerial earns its keep

Drone video pays when seeing the place is the buying decision. If your customers choose you by picturing themselves there, footage that lets them feel it before they arrive does real commercial work.

  • Venues, hospitality, and attractions. A flythrough lets a couple, a corporate planner, or a family walk your space from their phone. Tours get shorter, and the people who do visit arrive ready to book.
  • Builders, contractors, and anyone selling scale. Some work only reads from the air: the size of the site, the quality of the build, the setting around it. Ground photos flatten exactly the thing you are proudest of.
  • Events and race days. The recap film is next year's ticket sales team, and the sponsor cut is how backers see their money working. Coverage that produces both turns one day into a season of marketing.
  • Businesses whose setting is the product. Golf courses, campuses, dealerships, marinas, main street districts. If the location is why people come, the location should be the film.

Where it usually does not

We would rather tell you this before you spend than after. Aerial video is a weak buy when your customers choose you on referral, credentials, or a one to one conversation, and the place itself is not part of the decision. It is also a weak buy as a rescue: one beautiful video cannot fix an offer, a website, or a booking process that is losing people for other reasons. And if the film would live in exactly one post and never be seen again, the math rarely works. Which brings up the point most buyers miss.

One shoot should feed months of content

The value is rarely the single hero film. A single well planned shoot can produce the homepage film, short social cuts for a season of posts, sponsor ready clips, a recruiting piece, and ad material, all from the same flights. Judged as one video, aerial can look expensive. Judged as the asset library it actually is, the cost per use drops fast, and the film keeps working long after the invoice is forgotten.

Three questions before you spend anything

  • Do my customers decide by seeing the place, the day, or the scale of what we do?
  • Will the film live where buyers actually decide: my homepage, my Google profile, my booking page, my sponsors' feeds?
  • Can one shoot feed several channels, or am I buying a single post?

Three yes answers, and aerial video is very likely worth it for a business like yours. Mostly no answers, and your money is probably better spent elsewhere first. Either way, you now know, and knowing before the quote is the whole point.
