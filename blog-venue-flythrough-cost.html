How Much Does a Venue Flythrough Cost? (and What Moves It)

You want a number before you want a conversation, and you are right to. Here is the published range for a venue flythrough, the honest reasons one venue lands at the bottom of it and another at the top, and the point at which a quote should start making you suspicious.

March 2026

The published range, in plain words

FPV flythroughs start from $1,250, and most single location flythroughs land between $1,250 and $2,500. Those are the figures published on this site, and they are the only flythrough figures we will give you, here or on a call, until a written quote prices your specific project. Anyone who gives you a firmer number before knowing your space is guessing with your money.

That range covers a real, finished product: flight planning, the filming visit, a finished master film, and short social cutdowns. It is not a teaser rate for raw clips with the edit sold later. When you compare a quote against it, compare what is included first and the total second, because the cheapest flythrough in Omaha is usually the one that quietly excludes the edit, the planning, or the usage rights.

For wider context, listing photography packages in this market run between $95 and $500 locally, and they are a different, commodity product: quick stills and clips per property, not a planned one take film of a venue. If that is all your space needs, buy that and save the difference. A flythrough is priced as a venue asset that works for seasons, and it should be judged that way. What moves a quote inside the range

  • Route length and complexity. A single building with one clear guest route sits near the bottom. Multiple buildings, levels, indoor and outdoor sections, and tight or rehearsed transitions take more planning and more takes.
  • Location and airspace. A site outside Gretna with open sky is simple to plan. A downtown venue near the RiverFront, under busier airspace and closer to Eppley Airfield, needs more authorisation and permission work before anyone flies.
  • Filming window difficulty. A venue that can offer a quiet dressed window is straightforward. A working restaurant, hotel, or campus that must be filmed in narrow seams between services takes more visits or more patience, and patience is a cost like any other.
  • Deliverables. One master film is the base. Extra cutdowns, separate edits of individual spaces for planners or departments, and versions for different channels each add edit time.
  • Turnaround and usage. Standard delivery is within five business days. A date fixed launch, a season opening, an event reveal, can justify faster work, and broader or longer usage rights are worth more than narrow ones. Both belong in the open in the quote.

Notice what does not move the price: the prestige of your venue, the size of your marketing budget, or how keen you sound on the phone. A quote that rises because a venue looks wealthy is not a quote, it is a guess about what you will tolerate. The factors above are the legitimate levers, and you are entitled to ask which of them placed your project where it landed.

Why quotes from different operators spread so widely

Flythrough quotes vary because the word covers several different jobs. At the low end, a quote may describe a hobbyist with a drone, raw footage, no route planning, no permission work, and no usage terms. In the middle sits the finished product described above. Above the published range, a quote is usually describing a production: multiple filming days, a crew, styled sets, actors or full event dressing, and a campaign edit. Those can be legitimate. They are simply not the same purchase.

The format comparison in our guide to flythroughs, 360 tours, and regular video helps here too, because some quotes that look expensive for a flythrough turn out to be priced for a different format altogether. Match the product first. Then judge the price.

What your written quote should list

Every written quote from us lists deliverables, delivery date, and usage rights, line by line, and you should require the same from anyone. For a flythrough, a complete quote also names the location check outcome, the filming window, what happens if weather forces a move, and whether cutdowns are included or extra. If a quote is one number with none of that written down, the risk sits with you when the day or the edit disappoints.

Here is the honesty sentence this page owes you: for a small, single room space that photographs clearly, even the bottom of our range is probably too much to spend, and a quote at any price from us would be the wrong advice. The range exists for venues whose layout, arrival, and flow are the product. If yours is not one of them, the money belongs elsewhere, and the range request below will get you that answer in writing rather than a sales call.

Reading your own quote with fresh eyes

Before you accept any flythrough quote, including ours, ask three things. Does it state which end of the published range you are in and why, in factors you can verify? Does it include the finished film and cutdowns, or will those appear later as extras? And does it say, in writing, what you may use the film for and for how long? Three clear answers mean you are comparing a product. Anything less means you are comparing a promise.
