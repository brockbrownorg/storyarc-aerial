Your First Drone Shoot, Step by Step: From First Call to Final File

Most of the anxiety in a first drone booking comes from not knowing what happens next, or what will be asked of you, or when. The process is actually short and orderly. Here it is in full, so you can see every step before you take the first one.

December 2025

Steps one to three: the conversation before any commitment

Step one is a short description from you. What you want filmed, where it is, and when you need it. A few sentences are enough. You do not need a shot list, a script, or the right vocabulary. Describing the outcome you want, more tours booked, a sponsor shown their value, a homepage that finally explains your place, is more useful than describing camera moves.

Step two is the location and airspace check, which is free. Your address decides more than buyers expect. A property outside Gretna and a property in the Old Market near Eppley Airfield sit in different airspace and under different permission needs. We look at your specific place, check what applies, and tell you plainly whether the flight you imagine can happen as imagined. If it cannot, we say what version of it can.

Step three is a written quote. The quote lists deliverables, delivery date, and usage rights, along with the time on site and the weather terms. Our flythrough range, from $1,250 with most single location projects between $1,250 and $2,500, gives you a sense of scale before the quote arrives, and the full Omaha cost guide explains what moves a project inside its range. Nothing is booked until you accept the written quote. Reading it commits you to nothing.

Steps four and five: planning and the shoot day

Step four is planning, and most of it is ours to do. We build the flight path or shot list, confirm access and timing with the venue, watch the weather as the day approaches, and agree the small list of things we need from you, which the companion guides cover and which is shorter than most first time buyers fear. You will know when we arrive, how long we expect to be there, and what the space needs to look like.

Step five is the shoot itself. Most single location flythroughs film in one to two hours. We arrive, walk the route once on the ground, make small adjustments for light and for whatever the day has placed in the way, and fly the planned lines, usually more than once so the edit has choices. Event coverage runs longer by design, booked as a half day or full day block. Either way, your working day keeps working. A venue in Papillion does not close for a flythrough, and a business in Omaha does not empty its calendar for a camera.

Weather is the one honest variable. Nebraska wind can rise by midday and summer storms can end an afternoon early. If the day turns, we make the call with you early and rescheduling never punishes the buyer. That policy is in the quote, not offered as a favor later.

Steps six and seven: the edit and the final file

Step six is the edit. The best passes are selected, ordered, colored, and set to music, and the social cutdowns are made from the same material. Finished films are delivered within five business days as our standard, and production files within two business days when an editor is waiting on the footage. The delivery date in your quote is the promise to plan your launch around.

Step seven is delivery and review. You receive the finished film and the named cutdowns, in the formats the quote listed, with your usage rights restated in plain words. You review, you use the revision rounds the quote includes, and then the film goes to work on your homepage, your Google profile, your booking page, and your social channels. That is also the moment most buyers realize one shoot has stocked several months of posts, which is a fine realization to have with the invoice already closed.

The sentence that costs us: some first inquiries end at step two, with us reporting that a location cannot be flown the way a buyer hoped, or that the honest answer is to wait, prepare the space, or spend the money elsewhere first. Those answers end the sale, and they are still the right step two outcome.

Start at step one whenever you like through the quote page. The first steps cost nothing but a few sentences.

A word about your own role across these steps, because first time buyers often overestimate it. You are not project managing this. Your moments are front loaded and brief: the description, the address, access on the day, and a look at the finished film when it arrives. The long middle, planning, permissions, weather judgment, flying, and editing, belongs to the operator you hired, and a process that keeps asking you to carry that middle has been designed for the operator convenience rather than yours. Expect the calm shape described here, and treat a process that feels chaotic at step one as reliable information about how step five will feel.
