How integration works

Four steps, built to sit inside the process you already run.

01

Creative Call

Thirty minutes with your director or DP. References, boards, and the job the aerial has to do in the story.

02

Shot Design

Flight paths drawn against the location, the light, and your ground coverage. Airspace and venue rules checked now, not on the day.

03

On call sheet time, briefed, and flying in sync with your unit. Takes logged so post knows exactly what it has.

04

Questions from production teams

How far ahead should we book?

As early as you can. A week or more lets us scout properly and design the flight paths with you. When the schedule allows, we can move faster, and we will tell you honestly if we cannot.

What do you need from us before the shoot?

The call sheet, your shot list or boards, location details, and one point of contact on the day. Everything else, airspace checks, flight planning, and gear, is on us.

Can you match our look?

Yes. Camera settings and color approach are agreed in the creative call, and we shoot test frames against your ground footage when the production calls for it.

What happens if the weather turns?

We watch the forecast with you starting days out. If flying conditions fall apart, we reschedule the aerial block rather than force it, and the rest of your day keeps moving.

What about rules at our location?

Every location carries its own airspace and venue rules. We sort out which ones apply during planning and flag any shot that needs adjusting early, while there is still time to design around it. More on our safety and rules page.

Do you only fly, or can you edit the sequence too?

Both. Many teams want clean footage for their own editor. Others hand us the aerial block to cut. Tell us which on the consult call and the quote will reflect it.
